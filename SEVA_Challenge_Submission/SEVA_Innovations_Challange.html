01. Problem and Location

Statement: A specific, observed problem in a named place.

A. Portal Copy-Paste Version (964 / 1000 Characters)

In rural agricultural blocks of West Bengal (Lower Gangetic Plains Zone III: Hooghly, Purba Bardhaman, Nadia, Bankura, Jhargram, and South 24 Parganas/Sundarbans), alongside adjoining districts in Bihar and Jharkhand, over 86% of farmers are small and marginal landholders cultivating Aman/Boro paddy, potato, jute, mustard, and vegetables. Field observations show farmers suffer 15-25% annual crop yield losses (ICAR data) due to three critical gaps: (1) delayed diagnosis of fast-spreading blights (Potato/Tomato Late Blight) and insect outbreaks (Brown Planthopper, Yellow Stem Borer, Fall Armyworm); (2) lack of last-mile internet connectivity in field plots and high extension worker deficits (>1:1,500 farmers); and (3) miscalculated chemical fertilizer mixing (e.g., failing to deduct 18% N supplied by DAP when adding Urea, or mixing Zinc Sulphate with DAP causing insoluble zinc phosphate fixation). B. Full Comprehensive Section Content

In the agrarian heartland of the Eastern Region of India—specifically observed across the Lower Gangetic Plains Region (Agro-Climatic Zone III) in West Bengal (spanning the intensive rice-potato-jute-vegetable belts of Hooghly, Purba Bardhaman, Nadia, Murshidabad, the Red & Laterite uplands of Bankura, Purulia, and Jhargram, the Terai tea-and-vegetable belt of Cooch Behar and Jalpaiguri, and the Coastal Saline belt of South 24 Parganas / Sundarbans), as well as the adjoining Middle Gangetic Plains (Zone IV) of Bihar (Purnia, Katihar, Muzaffarpur, Samastipur, Nalanda) and the Eastern Plateau & Hills (Zone VII) of Jharkhand (Ranchi, Hazaribagh, Dumka, West Singhbhum)—agriculture serves as the primary livelihood for over 65% of the rural population. Across these three Eastern Region states, more than 86% to 92% of operational farm holdings belong to small and marginal farmers cultivating fragmented land parcels typically smaller than 1 hectare (approximately 2.47 acres or 7.47 Standard Bengal Bighas).

Despite cultivating high-value multi-crop sequences—such as Aman Paddy (Swarna/MTU-7029, Gobindobhog, Shatabdi) followed by Rabi Potato (Kufri Jyoti in Hooghly and Purba Bardhaman) or Rapeseed-Mustard (Toria/Sarson) and Summer Boro Paddy or Pre-Kharif Jute (Tossa/White)—smallholder farmers in these named blocks repeatedly experience 15% to 25% preventable annual crop yield losses (as documented by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research, ICAR) and lose 20% to 35% of their net cultivation income due to unnecessary or improperly timed chemical input expenditure. Direct field evaluations across village farm plots reveal five deeply interconnected, location-specific structural problems:

  • Catastrophic Crop Losses from Delayed Disease & Pest Identification at the Field Edge:

During humid monsoon (Kharif) and foggy winter (Rabi) spells in West Bengal, Bihar, and Jharkhand, fungal, bacterial, and viral pathogens spread exponentially within 48 to 72 hours. In Hooghly and Purba Bardhaman potato fields, *Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans) and Early Blight (Alternaria solani) can decimate an entire standing crop within 4–5 days of water-soaked dark lesion onset if prophylactic fungicides (such as Mancozeb 75% WP @ 2.5 g/L or Cymoxanil + Mancozeb) are not sprayed immediately. Similarly, in Aman and Boro paddy tracts across Nadia, Bardhaman, and Bihar's Kosi basin, Brown Planthopper (BPH — Nilaparvata lugens) causes sudden circular patches of "hopper burn," while Yellow Stem Borer (Scirpophaga incertulas) and Rice Leaf Roller (Cnaphalocrocis medinalis) cause severe dead-hearts and white-ears. In maize and vegetable tracts across Jharkhand and North Bengal, Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda), Fruit & Shoot Borers, Red Spider Mites, and Aphids* inflict massive damage. Because farmers cannot distinguish early-stage disease lesions or identify exact Economic Threshold Levels (ETL) in the field, they either wait until irreversible foliar destruction occurs or rely on local pesticide shop dealers who frequently prescribe overpriced, broad-spectrum, or unscientific chemical cocktails.

  • Severe Extension Bottlenecks and Rural Connectivity Dead-Zones (Air-Gapped Field Conditions):

In rural panchayats across Jhargram, Bankura, the Sundarbans islands, Santhal Parganas (Jharkhand), and rural Bihar, the ratio of government agricultural extension officers (Krishi Prayukti Sahayaks / Block Technology Managers) to cultivating farmers exceeds 1:1,500 to 1:2,500. Physical visits to distant Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs) cost a smallholder farmer an entire day's wage and transport fare. Meanwhile, existing smartphone agricultural apps depend entirely on high-speed 4G/5G cloud servers to upload high-resolution leaf photos and run cloud AI inference. In deep agricultural fields, riverine char lands, forest-fringe tribal blocks, and coastal embankments, mobile data speeds drop to zero or intermittent 2G, rendering cloud-dependent diagnostic apps completely unusable precisely where crops are grown.

  • Unbalanced Fertilizer Application and Chemical Incompatibility Mistakes:

Although millions of Soil Health Cards (SHC) measuring 12 soil parameters (pH, EC, OC, N, P, K, S, Zn, B, Fe, Mn, Cu) have been issued, smallholder farmers struggle to translate laboratory values (kg/ha of elemental N, P₂O₅, K₂O and ppm of micronutrients) into commercial bags and kilograms of Neem-Coated Urea (46% N), DAP (18% N, 46% P₂O₅), SSP (16% P₂O₅, 11% S), and MOP (60% K₂O) per acre or per Bengal Bigha. Crucially, farmers routinely apply full doses of Urea without deducting the 18% Nitrogen already supplied by basal DAP, leading to excessive vegetative growth that attracts Brown Planthopper and bacterial blight while leaching nitrates into groundwater. Furthermore, in acidic red, lateritic, and Terai soils (pH < 5.5 - 6.5 in Jhargram, Bankura, Jalpaiguri, and Jharkhand) where Zinc and Boron deficiencies are rampant, farmers frequently tank-mix or physically blend Zinc Sulphate Heptahydrate (ZnSO₄·7H₂O) directly with DAP or SSP, triggering an immediate chemical reaction that precipitates insoluble Zinc Phosphate, neutralizing both expensive inputs.

  • Language, Literacy, and Technical Jargon Barriers:

Most digital advisory portals deliver text-heavy outputs in English or complex bureaucratic terminology. Elderly farmers, women agricultural workers, and tribal cultivators speaking Bengali (বাংলা), Hindi (हिन्दी), Odia (ଓଡ଼ିଆ), Maithili (मैथिली), Santali (ᱥᱟᱱᱛᱟᱲᱤ), Kurmali, or Urdu require voice-first, zero-jargon guidance that speaks and listens in their native tongue and provides exact dosages in local units (grams per liter of water, kilograms per acre/bigha) along with immediate awareness of the PMFBY 72-hour localized calamity reporting rule after hailstorms or unseasonal cyclonic rains.

B. Full Comprehensive Section Content

We have designed, built, and validated Krishi Mitra (कृषि मित्र / কৃষি মিত্র)—a 100% offline, air-gapped, multilingual on-device Artificial Intelligence agricultural diagnostic and advisory system engineered to run on a portable, low-power Raspberry Pi 5 (Quad-Core Arm Cortex-A76, 4GB RAM) or a local Panchayat/FPO field laptop. Instead of requiring farmers to have internet connectivity or pay for cloud subscriptions, Krishi Mitra broadcasts a local Wi-Fi hotspot (0.0.0.0:8000) at the village Gram Panchayat, Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS), Bangla Sahayata Kendra (BSK), Farmer Producer Organization (FPO) center, or mobile extension van. Any farmer within range can open the application on any basic smartphone browser or interact directly via a touchscreen kiosk—with zero mobile data, zero SIM card, and zero internet dependency.

Described plainly, Krishi Mitra combines five practical, farmer-facing tools into a single daylight-readable interface (designed in high-contrast White, Dark Navy Blue #0B192C, and Royal Harvest Gold #D4AF37, completely free of confusing AI jargon or emojis):

  • Two-Stage Crop & Flower Disease Scanner (42 Agricultural Classes + Non-Crop Gatekeeper):

A farmer captures or uploads a photograph of an affected plant leaf or flower head (covering major crops including Paddy/Rice, Potato, Tomato, Corn/Maize, Wheat, Sunflower, Cotton, Chilli, Bell Pepper, Apple, Grape, etc.) or selects from handcrafted crop vector SVG tiles.

  • Stage 1 (Smart Photo Verification Gatekeeper): Before running disease diagnosis, an on-device zero-shot vision filter verifies whether the photo actually depicts a plant leaf, crop foliage, or flower. If a farmer accidentally uploads a photo of a person, face, vehicle, furniture, or blank background, the system immediately halts and asks for a clear leaf photo (is_crop: False), eliminating absurd false alarms.
  • Stage 2 (Disease Diagnosis, Quality Grade & Severity): For valid crop photos, the system identifies the exact disease condition (e.g., Late Blight — Water-Soaked Dark Rot, Early Blight, Common Rust, Gray Mold, or Healthy Leaf Condition), assigns an intuitive Crop Quality Grade (Grade A: Prime, Grade B: Attention Required, Grade C: Health Risk), calculates a Health Score out of 100, classifies Severity (None, Mild, Moderate, Critical), and outputs step-by-step cultural, organic, and chemical remedies with exact dosage rates per liter of water and per acre. To prevent confusing farmers when an image is blurry or ambiguous, a strict UI rule automatically hides the numerical accuracy percentage whenever diagnostic confidence is below 80%.
  • Field Insect Pest Detector & 102-Class IPM Classifier:

By toggling to the Field Pest Scanner, farmers can photograph insect pests or pest-damaged stems/whorls. An on-device object detection and classification engine trained on the IP102 agricultural pest dataset detects and draws bounding boxes around 97 to 102 pest species (including Brown Planthopper, Rice Leaf Roller, Yellow Rice Borer, Corn Borer, Beet/Cabbage Armyworm, Aphids, Mole Cricket, and Red Spider Mites). For every detected pest, Krishi Mitra provides a complete Integrated Pest Management (IPM) card:

  • Economic Threshold Level (ETL): Tells the farmer when chemical spraying is economically justified (e.g., "5–10 hoppers per hill at vegetative stage" for BPH or "1–2 freshly rolled leaves per hill" for Rice Leaf Roller).
  • Organic & Biological RemedyFirst: Recommends 5% Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE), Neem Oil @ 5 ml/L, Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt) @ 2 g/L, Beauveria bassiana @ 5 g/L, or Trichogramma egg parasitoid cards @ 50,000/ha.
  • Targeted Chemical Remedy & Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI): Prescribes exact ICAR-approved molecules and dosages (e.g., Chlorantraniliprole 18.5% SC @ 0.4 ml/L or Triflumezopyrim 10% SC @ 0.5 ml/L) and cultural practices (30 cm alleyways in paddy, pheromone traps @ 5/acre).
  • 12-Parameter Soil Health Card (SHC) Commercial Fertilizer & Amendment Calculator:

Farmers enter values from their government Soil Health Card across all 12 parameters: Soil pH, Electrical Conductivity (EC in dS/m), Organic Carbon (OC %), Primary Macronutrients (N, P₂O₅, K₂O in kg/ha), Secondary Nutrient Sulphur (S in ppm), and Micronutrients (Zinc, Boron, Iron, Manganese, Copper in ppm), along with their target crop (18+ crop profiles including Aman Rice, Boro Rice, Potato, Mustard, Jute, Wheat, Maize, Groundnut, Chickpea, Tomato, Brinjal, Chilli, Cabbage, Okra, Sunflower, Sugarcane) and farm area in acres.

  • The calculator automatically applies STCR (Soil Test Crop Response) adjustments (+25% if nutrient is Low, 100% if Medium, -25% if High).
  • It computes exact commercial quantities in both kilograms and standard commercial bags (45 kg Neem-Coated Urea bags and 50 kg DAP/SSP/MOP bags), automatically deducting the 18% Nitrogen supplied by basal DAP from the Urea requirement.
  • It prescribes exact Agricultural Lime (CaCO₃) for acidic soils (pH < 6.5), Agricultural Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) for alkaline/sodic soils (pH > 7.5) and groundnut pegging, Zinc Sulphate (21% Zn) when Zn < 0.6 ppm, and Borax (10.5% B) when B < 0.5 ppm.
  • It generates a Stage-Wise Split Schedule (Basal, Active Tillering/CRI/Earthing-Up, Panicle Initiation/Flowering) and enforces a mandatory safety warning: "Do NOT mix Zinc Sulphate directly with DAP or SSP in the same container."
  • Multilingual Offline Voice & Chat Assistant Grounded in 122 ICAR Chunks:

Farmers can tap the microphone button to speak their question in Bengali, Hindi, Punjabi, Telugu, Tamil, Marathi, Odia, Maithili, Santali, Urdu, or English (supporting all 22 Scheduled Indian Languages + English) and listen to the diagnosis or advisory read aloud via Text-to-Speech (TTS). Every response is grounded strictly in 122 verified ICAR, TNAU, BCKV/UBKV, IMD, and NFSM knowledge chunks stored on disk.

  • Offline Government Agricultural Schemes & Emergency Helpline Directory:

Provides step-by-step eligibility, document checklists, and action guides for PM-KISAN (Rs 6,000/yr + e-KYC rules), PMFBY Crop Insurance (highlighting the mandatory 72-hour localized calamity reporting rule via helpline 14447), Kisan Credit Card (KCC) (4% effective interest up to Rs 1.60 Lakh without collateral), Soil Health Card Scheme, e-NAM, and the 24x7 toll-free Kisan Call Centre (1800-180-1551).

03. Technology or Method

(2) Reasoning & RAG Pipeline: Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct quantized to INT4 (1.15GB RAM, 14.5 tok/s) runs on PyTorch ExecuTorch with Arm KleidiAI & XNNPACK SIMD kernels (:8008) / Ollama (:11434), grounded in 122 ICAR chunks in local ChromaDB (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings). Built with FastAPI, deterministic STCR soil math engine, offline Whisper-tiny ASR, and React 19 + TypeScript SPA.

B. Full Comprehensive Section Content

Krishi Mitra is engineered around a decoupled, multi-stage on-device edge AI architecture designed to operate within the strict memory and thermal envelope of a 4GB RAM Raspberry Pi 5 (Quad-Core 64-bit Arm Cortex-A76 @ 2.4GHz) with optional Raspberry Pi AI HAT+ (Hailo-8L / Hailo-10H NPU, 13–26 TOPS) or a local field station laptop (NVIDIA RTX 3050 4GB VRAM / CPU). At application startup (server/app/main.py and server/app/config.py), the system enforces strict air-gap environment flags (os.environ["HF_HUB_OFFLINE"] = "1", os.environ["TRANSFORMERS_OFFLINE"] = "1", os.environ["KERAS_BACKEND"] = "torch") so that 0.0 bytes of external network traffic are ever requested.

The technical methodology is structured into four core subsystems:

  • Subsystem A: Decoupled Multi-Model Computer Vision & Pest Detection Pipeline (server/app/vision/)

To prevent memory contention between vision transformers, convolutional networks, and language models on edge hardware, vision inference is isolated into an on-demand thread-safe pipeline:

  • Layer 1 — Zero-Shot Non-Crop Gatekeeper (vision_pipeline.py): First performs micro-dimension (<= 2x2 px) and flat-variance/channel-dominance checks (std_val < 3.0 and green-channel offset mean_g > mean_r + 15), followed by zero-shot multimodal contrast via google/siglip-base-patch16-224 (local_files_only=True). It compares the image embedding against agricultural text prompts ("an agricultural crop, plant leaf, foliage, fruit, sunflower petal, or flower blossom") versus non-crop prompts ("a person, human, face, body, gymnast, animal, car, building, or furniture"). Non-crop images are rejected with is_crop=False before disease models are invoked.
  • Layer 2 — Multi-Backbone Crop Disease & Blossom Grading (vision_pipeline.py, label_mapper.py, & AgriRakshak_Models_20261118_0437/Disease_Classification/): Routes general crops to a 38-class Vision Transformer (kimcomehome/plantvillage-vit-leaf-disease) alongside our custom-trained efficientnetb0_disease_primary.keras (38-class EfficientNetB0 backbone running on PyTorch backend in ~303–476 ms on CPU), and routes sunflower queries to a specialized 4-class YaswanthReddy23/ViT_Sunflower model (detecting Gray Mold, Downy Mildew, Leaf Scars, and Fresh Healthy Blossoms). Raw logits are processed via softmax and mapped through LabelMapper into structured agronomic objects containing crop_name, detected_condition, confidence, quality_score (0–100), quality_grade (Grade A/B/C), severity (None/Mild/Moderate/Critical), plain-language summaries, and ICAR treatment steps.
  • Layer 3 — Dual-Model IP102 Insect Pest Detection & Classification (pest_pipeline.py & AgriRakshak_Models_20261118_0437/):
  • Object Detection (Localization & Bounding Boxes): Uses our custom-trained YOLO26n checkpoint (Pest_Detection/best.pt, trained across 97 IP102 agricultural pest classes via Ultralytics on PyTorch). It returns exact [x1, y1, x2, y2] bounding box coordinates, class IDs, confidence scores, and multi-pest counts per image in 313 ms to 778 ms.
  • Fast Species Classification: Uses our custom-trained EfficientNetB0 checkpoint (Pest_Classification/best.keras, Experiment B01, covering all 102 IP102 pest classes defined in ip102_classes.txt), resizing inputs to 224x224 and executing a direct PyTorch tensor forward pass in 303 ms to 444 ms to output Top-K species candidates mapped to the IPM_ADVISORY_CATALOG.
  • Subsystem B: On-Device Llama 3.2 Reasoning via PyTorch ExecuTorch & Arm KleidiAI (llama/executorch/)
  • Model & Quantization: Uses meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct-Q4_K_M.gguf / .pte) quantized with 4-bit weight-only quantization (int4_weight_only). This compresses the model's runtime memory footprint to 1,150 MB (1.15 GB RAM), leaving over 2.8 GB of free RAM on a 4GB Raspberry Pi 5 for the OS, ChromaDB, and vision weights, while generating ~14.5 tokens/second on quad-core Arm Cortex-A76.
  • ExecuTorch & KleidiAI Acceleration: export_llama_pte.py lowers the PyTorch graph via XNNPACK and Arm KleidiAI SIMD vector micro-kernels optimized for Armv8.2-A Neon/dotprod instructions. llama_arm_runner.py and service.py expose a dedicated local inference microservice on http://127.0.0.1:8008/generate.
  • 3-Tier Resilient Generation Cascade (rag_pipeline.py): When a query arrives, RAGPipeline.ask() attempts: (Tier 1) On-device ExecuTorch service (:8008) → (Tier 2) Local Ollama LLM service (:11434) → (Tier 3) Deterministic Native-Language ICAR Synthesis Engine (_build_fallback_advisory), guaranteeing 100% uptime even if the LLM microservice is cold-starting.
  • Subsystem C: Grounded ChromaDB Vector Store & Deterministic STCR Soil Engine (server/app/rag/)
  • ChromaDB Persistence (chroma_store.py & knowledge_ingestion.py): Ingests Advisory data/india_farmer_advisory_rag_knowledge_base.md into 122 semantically chunked documents indexed on disk (server/chroma_db/chroma.sqlite3) using local sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings.
  • STCR 12-Parameter Soil Math Engine (soil_calculator.py): Implements exact quantitative soil chemistry classifiers:
  • pH Classifier: < 5.5 Strongly Acidic (400 kg/ha Lime), 5.5–6.5 Moderately Acidic (300 kg/ha Lime), 6.5–7.5 Neutral, 7.5–8.5 Moderately Alkaline (400 kg/ha Gypsum + Dhaincha), > 8.5 Sodic/Alkali (600 kg/ha Gypsum + leaching).
  • STCR Multipliers: Adjusts crop-specific Base RDF (N : P₂O₅ : K₂O) across 18+ crop profiles by 1.25x (Low: N < 280, P₂O₅ < 23, K₂O < 120 kg/ha), 1.00x (Medium), or 0.75x (High: N > 560, P₂O₅ > 57, K₂O > 280 kg/ha).
  • Commercial Fertilizer Stoichiometry: Calculates DAP (kg) = Field P_2O_5 / 0.46, computes N_DAP = DAP × 0.18, deducts N_DAP from total required Nitrogen (Remaining N = \max(0, Field N - N_DAP)), and computes Urea (kg) = Remaining N / 0.46, MOP (kg) = Field K_2O / 0.60, and SSP (kg) = Field P_2O_5 / 0.16.
  • Subsystem D: Offline Whisper Voice ASR, 22-Language Engine & Zero-Jargon Guardrails
  • Voice & Multilingual (voice.py & multilingual.py): Uses offline openai/whisper-tiny on CPU with a 12-second timeout for speech-to-text transcription across .wav/.webm/.ogg/.mp3 voice recordings, paired with browser SpeechSynthesisUtterance using native BCP-47 locale codes (bn-IN, hi-IN, or-IN, pa-IN, te-IN, ta-IN, mr-IN, etc.) across all 22 Scheduled Languages.
  • Safety & Anti-Leakage Filters: Regex blacklists (LEAKAGE_BLACKLIST_PATTERNS), probing interceptors (PROBING_KEYWORDS), out-of-domain filters (NON_AGRI_KEYWORDS), and emoji strippers (EMOJI_PATTERN) ensure zero internal model names or technical terms ever leak to the farmer.
